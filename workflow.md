# Executive Morning Brief Workflow

This page documents the process behind the Executive Morning Brief and how I used Microsoft Copilot to turn recurring executive-prep work into a scheduled workflow.

## 1. Identify the Information Sources

The workflow was designed to review the sources I already used when preparing for a morning executive meeting:

- CEO email activity from the previous 48 hours
- Read and unread messages
- Flagged and pinned emails
- Teams messages from the previous 48 hours
- The CEO's calendar for the current day

The goal was to reduce the amount of manual searching required before each morning meeting.

## 2. Define What Actually Matters

Rather than asking Copilot for a general summary, I gave it specific instructions about what information to surface.

The brief needed to identify:

- Emails that appeared to require a response
- Sender and subject for those emails
- Urgent items that had not already been answered
- A short draft response for urgent unanswered messages
- Meetings and calls scheduled for that day
- Meeting times
- Tasks or deadlines mentioned in recent emails
- Tasks or deadlines contained in calendar invitations

This helped keep the output focused on actionable information rather than producing a long summary of everything that happened.

## 3. Structure the Output for Fast Review

I asked Copilot to format the brief using clear sections and bulleted lists.

The output was designed to be scanned quickly before my daily meeting with the CEO rather than read like a report.

The main sections included:

- Emails Requiring Attention
- Today's Meetings & Calls
- Tasks & Deadlines

The exact wording and structure could be adjusted as I refined the prompt.

## 4. Refine the Prompt

I tested the prompt and reviewed the results to see whether Copilot was surfacing the right information.

I adjusted the instructions when the output was too broad, missed relevant details, or included information that was not useful for the morning meeting.

The goal was to create a repeatable output that required less cleanup over time.

## 5. Schedule the Workflow

Once the prompt and output format were working consistently, I scheduled the prompt to run every morning before the workday began.

This meant the briefing was ready before my daily executive meeting instead of requiring me to manually repeat the same review process each morning.

## 6. Review Before Use

The briefing was a preparation tool, not an automatic decision-maker.

Before using the information, I still applied human judgment to:

- Confirm accuracy
- Determine what was actually urgent
- Consider confidentiality and sensitivity
- Decide what should be escalated
- Adjust drafted responses when needed
- Add context Copilot may not have had

## What Was Automated vs. What Stayed Human

### Automated

- Reviewing recent communication
- Pulling relevant calendar information
- Surfacing potential tasks and deadlines
- Organizing information into a consistent format
- Drafting initial responses for urgent messages
- Running the workflow on a recurring schedule

### Human

- Judgment
- Prioritization
- Context
- Confidentiality decisions
- Final communication
- Executive escalation

## Why This Worked

The workflow did not try to automate executive support itself.

It automated the repetitive information-gathering layer so I could spend more of my time on judgment, preparation, and follow-through.
